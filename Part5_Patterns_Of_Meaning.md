# Patterns of Meaning

## The grain beneath the states — eigenforms, basins, and what stays when everything moves

## 1 · The whole thing in one sentence

> Love and grief, on this reading, are two slopes of one basin. What they drain toward keeps its
> shape however the feeling changes, and it has no name. That is the grain.

Part 1 gave the four seeds and the states they combine into. Part 4 gave the stances a perceiver
takes toward a moment. This part is about the thing both of those were built to find: the pattern
that stays when everything else moves, met from different angles and still recognizable. The
borrowed word is *eigenform*. The plain word is *the grain*. Reading a moment as love or grief or
longing is the surface of the notation — real, useful, and not the target. The target is the shape
underneath that generates those readings and is none of them.

**A lens, not a law.** Everything below is held at that strength, and §11 says exactly how strong.

## 2 · What stays when everything moves

A whirlpool holds its shape while the water moves through it. The water is the flux. The shape is the
eigenform. You are not cataloguing the water; you are looking for the shape that stays.

The word comes from Heinz von Foerster — *objects are tokens for eigen-behaviors* — and from Louis
Kauffman, who explained it as *a token for those behaviors that lend the object its apparent
stability* and gave it a formal home: an object is a fixed point of an observer's recursion. Look
again, and again, and what survives every look is the object. *Eigen* is German for *own*; in linear
algebra an eigenvector is the direction a transformation cannot turn, only scale. Part 1 §6
introduced the term; here it is the subject.

Three sentences carry the whole idea. The first two restate Part 1 §6.

- **States tell you what you observe in a moment. Invariants tell you what persists across
  observations.** The same situation generates many valid state-readings; the grain is the structure
  beneath that generates all of them.
- **Meaning is a trajectory, not a position.** Not a point you locate or an architecture you draw,
  but a direction: where something is pointing. The archive this part is drawn from reads the order
  of the seeds as one such trajectory; Part 1 §3 is more careful and reads it as a root rather than a
  timeline. The ring of Part 4 is a different sequence again: the path work takes.
- **A name is a hypothesis about a grain, never the grain.** Which is why the archive labels what it
  finds Grain-1, Grain-2, and not with confident nouns.

Two more words do most of the work below. A *basin* is a region that readings settle into, the way
water settles into a valley; an *attractor* is the low point a basin drains toward. On the picture
this part develops, the seeds do not mark places. They shape a landscape, and the grains are its
deepest low points — inferred, never directly named. It is tempting to draw the notation as a fixed
structure, rooms or a lattice, and point at its parts; the archive did, and gave it up, because a
fixed drawing freezes a moving thing. The question underneath every reading is: what is the thing
that stays when everything else moves?

One caution before going on. Three different objects get called the grain in what follows, and they
are not the same: Kauffman's fixed point of an observer's recursion; the centre of a basin on the
landscape picture; and, from §6, a cycle the observation leaves invariant. §6 keeps them apart.

## 3 · Love and grief, and the centre with no name

Love, grief, care, memory, longing: on this reading these are not five things but five slopes of one
basin, and what lies at its centre has no word. The clearest map of that basin was drawn in June
2026, in a conversation with an outside model (ChatGPT), and it is worth printing whole. It is one
session's output, and it is the only basin the archive has actually mapped; §11 grades it
accordingly.

```
  centre                 (no word — provisional handle: attachment)
  ring 1 · felt          Love · Care · Memory · Grief · Longing
  ring 2 · structural    Commitment · Belonging · Protection · Loss · Nostalgia
  ring 3 · identity      Home · Family · Tradition · Legacy · Identity
  ring 4 · collective    Culture · History · Place · Meaning · Purpose
```

*The rings were numbered in the first map; the labels on them were added in a later note.*

Here is how I read it, from the centre out. Love and grief sit in the same innermost ring, not as
opposites but as neighbours: the same bond, one with the thing present and one with it gone. Care,
memory and longing are the same bond at three distances. One ring out, the words are the structures
those feelings build — commitment, belonging, loss. One more, and they are what those structures make
of a person — home, family, identity. At the rim they are what whole peoples make of them — culture,
history, place.

The claim is not that these twenty words are synonyms. It is that they are *contours* on one basin.
Each names a slope; none names the centre. The provisional handle for the centre is *attachment*, and
on my reading the handle is wrong in the way every handle is wrong: it is one more contour, a little
further in. What the archive has adopted is the discipline — the centre is inferred and never named
directly. A stronger note from June goes further, that the grain *cannot* be named, only pointed at,
and it stays a proposal, not adopted. It noticed the resemblance to the opening of the *Tao Te Ching*, that the way
which can be spoken is not the constant way, and to Wittgenstein's line between what can be said and
what can only be shown. A resemblance, not a proof.

Four things the session noted:

- **Words may describe contours rather than eigenforms.** Most of our vocabulary for feeling is
  slope-vocabulary.
- **Different perspectives can occupy different slopes of the same basin.** Two people in the same
  loss, one calling it grief and one calling it memory, are not disagreeing about the loss.
- **Deep basins are repeatedly rediscovered; shallow basins may be remembered rather than
  rediscovered.** A grain is the kind of thing you find again from cold. A convention is the kind of
  thing you have to be told.
- **The same geometry may recur at different scales.** Inside *love* the basin repeats: romantic love
  → lost love → grief → memory → nostalgia and longing; familial love opens onto protection, care,
  legacy. This is the fractal hypothesis. It has one sketched tree and no test.

An idea the archive added at the end of June says the same thing from the inside. Some pairs of
feelings *blend*: love and grief arrive together, and the mixture has its own name — bittersweet —
because the two share a basin. Other pairs the archive calls *conjugate*: having a thing and watching
it; being moved and being emptied. They are two vantages that cannot be occupied at the same moment.
(The word is used more loosely here than in the change-and-weight prediction of §6, which failed;
this softer claim is still held.)

Grief itself is written two ways in the archive. The adopted form, and the one Part 1 §2 prints, is
change with the good leaving, `F · V+v`: grief's badness is the loss of a positive, not a negative
value. A second reading, from June, keeps the weight where it was and removes the object instead:
grief as love with the object gone — the bond unchanged, the weight unchanged, the change high. It
makes a prediction, that in clean grief the bond does not turn into repulsion, and it names its own
falsifier: the complicated grief in which it does. One later note withdrew it as unwritable, since the
notation has no subtraction; another, the same day, holds it open. The notation can write a seed
absent, or fading (`Ov`). What it cannot write is *was there and is gone* as distinct from *never
there* — which is the severed anchor of §8, met inside a feeling.

That is the discipline in one picture: name the contours freely, hold the centre open, and treat
every name as a hypothesis the next session may improve.

## 4 · The terrain

The working model behind the map is small enough to print:

```
  states        points in experience
  transitions   paths between them
  basins        attractor regions around stable structures
  eigenforms    the deep basin-centres — inferred, never directly named
  the seeds     terrain-generating geology; not themselves basins
```

Having mapped one basin, the archive drew three more by symmetry, one for each remaining seed. An
object-basin, whose felt ring is recognition, clarity, curiosity, confusion, doubt, and whose rim is
science, law, language, dogma, paradox. A change-basin, from excitement, flow, restlessness, fear,
boredom out to progress, evolution, ritual, entropy, inertia. A meaning-basin, from joy, awe, desire,
dread, emptiness out to ethics, religion, art, nihilism, decadence. They are drawn, not found. The
archive grades them *edge, not floor*: derived by symmetry, unverified, not to be built on. I print
their rims so the shape is visible, and not so that they are believed.

Two claims came with the four-basin picture, and both have been demoted. One was that every basin
drains toward meaning — that the fourth seed's basin is the terminus of the others. That may be a
finding and it may be an artifact of the dependency chain, which already puts `V` last; the archive
grades it as possibly the second, and nothing yet distinguishes them. The other was that four appears
everywhere — the four elements, Aristotle's four causes, Jung's four functions, DNA's four bases — and
that this says something. It says that four is small. It was retired as numerology in July. (Part 1
§8 gives the deeper reason: the notation has a rule for combining seeds and none for composing them,
so *four* cannot be a result of it.)

One challenge stands rather than being demoted. Two of three outside model sessions, pressed on *Not a Fifth Thing* (the origin poem's compression, printed in Part 3), independently suggested that value may already live in relational structure
before anything is said to matter — which would make `V` less a terminus than a reading of the bond.
That is the old question of how *is* becomes *ought*, and it is open.

### The space the notation stands in

A dynamical system is a state space plus a field over it, and in June the archive rebuilt the
notation in exactly that shape. The four seeds are independent coordinates: every point is a valid
state, and a weight ringing loud over an object sitting near zero is simply a point in a corner of the
space. Over the space sits a landscape. **Standing** states are basins — the flow holds them.
**Floating** states are points in regions with no basin — the flow does not settle, the state is
transient, seeking. The order of the seeds is re-expressed here not as a rule the coordinates enforce
but as the *shape of the landscape*: the low-energy path runs along the chain, and the basins sit
where it is satisfied.

One worked example carries the picture. Start in a standing state: a clear object, a relation settled
around it. The object anchor shifts — `O` drops. As it falls past the basin's rim the point leaves
the basin; the relation loses what it was anchored to and goes open; the destabilization itself is `F`
rising. The point now sits in a low-`O`, high-`F`, unanchored region, and there is no basin there,
because with no object there is nothing for a relation or a change to stabilize around. The flow does
not settle. The file calls the result *drift* — change with no stable relation to hold it; by Part 1
§3's table, with the object gone as well, it sits closer to *chaos*, change with no form. The
landscape does not care which name; the point is that there is no basin there.

The resolution is the old grammar, mechanized: when a new distinction forms, there is a basin to catch
the point, and the trajectory falls in. The archive is careful about who does that. Completing a
floating state is the perceiver forming a new distinction, not the landscape growing one on its own.
Part 4 §8's two clocks say how: on the fast clock the perceiver selects among basins already latent
in the landscape; on the slow clock its accumulated history carves new ones.

A first version of the space was corrected within the day by an adversarial pass across models: the
claim that a relation plus a change could stand as a stable state without an object was withdrawn.
Without a stable object the downstream seeds compete — decay against spontaneous support — and none of
those configurations is a resting basin; they are transients passing through. Only fully anchored
configurations are basins. The power of the space is in which regions are *not* basins. A candidate
potential for the landscape was filed with three conditions before it could be adopted: restore the
signs, since as first written it could not tell love from contempt; leave re-anchoring to the
perceiver; hold it as a lens. A sign fix was adopted the next day, and the field stays a modelling choice. A later note proposes the next refinement and has not made it: standing and floating should
be a matter of degree — how deep a basin is — rather than a binary.

A word on *drift*, because the archive uses it three ways. Here it is a state: change with no stable
relation to hold it. In §8 it is a failure of reading: landing in the wrong basin because it was near
and it glowed. Elsewhere it is plain decay: structure that nobody tends changes on its own. The three
are related, and they are not the same thing.

## 5 · Any seed can be the grain

Part 1 §6 gives the rule — `O` is not the only seed that can be held steady; a weight held through
change is flow or siege, a process held is persistence — and the image: the sandstorm, and the rock it
leaves. Part 1's frame table already implies a third case — Stewardship conserves the committed bond, so *relation* can be the grain as well. And the image has a corollary worth saying aloud: a grain is only visible against something moving. Not despite the flux
— because of it.

A note coined in June carries the same move into a life. *Eigenform preservation* names the drive to hold a pattern steady — a self, a distinction — across flux. The note is about people whose contribution was the distinction itself, made against a world that kept saying they were someone else — Frederick Douglass, Anne Frank, Sappho — and about people who were misread while their contribution lay elsewhere, Galileo and Turing
among them. Its strongest case is Du Bois's double consciousness: being miscategorized until you can
see the category, which produces a new distinction rather than only guarding an old one. The note
calls it an engine, not a law, and carries its own brake: some people contributed for reasons that had
nothing to do with it.

The notation's own grain is a poem: the five-line short form of *A Mind, Turned* (Part 1 §10).

```
This, not that —
and the line is a meeting.
What changes carries what it changed from.
Not a fifth thing.
A mind, turned.
```

The archive's rebuild of the notation in June ran in phases — the space of §4, the operator of Part 4 §8, the dynamics of §6 — and took the poem as its fixed point: each phase was to re-compress to it. The first phase's round trip is on file, and the second sealed with the poem as its keystone; the third phase's re-compression gate is still open. That is a rule I keep, not evidence for the poem. A structure that failed to re-compress
would have been thrown out, so the poem could not lose. What the rule buys is a fixed point for the
rebuilds themselves — the grain doing, on the framework, the job this part describes.

## 6 · A fixed point, or a turning that returns

Part 4 §8 reported this in a paragraph, as it bears on the frames. Here is what it rests on.

Kauffman's definition makes a grain a fixed point: apply the observation — call it the operator, as Part 4 §8 does — again and nothing changes.
Part 1 keeps that reading, and so does §2 above. In June the archive tried to make it move, and four
things came of the attempt.

**The revision.** As I watched the notation run, the fixed-point picture stopped fitting what I could feel. The thing that holds in a whirlpool is not a point; every drop of water is moving. It is the
*cycle* that holds. So a grain was re-read as a cycle the operator leaves invariant: individual
states keep changing, and the shape persists because they do. *A mind, turned* is a turning that
returns, not a point that sits still. The rebuild's own file went further than a lens and retired the fixed point; this series keeps both, with Part 1's fixed point as the way in and the cycle over it.

**The triage.** Three objects — not quite §2's three — had been loosely equated along the way, and they are not synonyms: a
*fixed point* of the operator, which may be a saddle — balanced, but tipped off by any push — that you
cannot rest in; an *attractor* of a downhill flow, the bottom of an energy bowl, a basin of stasis;
and a *re-entrant form*, a shape that composes with itself. A high-energy transient can be a fixed
point of a badly tuned operator and never an attractor. Keeping the three apart is most of the clarity this section has. The attractor is §2's basin centre; the cycle meets the re-entrant form only by analogy (below).

**What the check found.** The first mechanism tried for the cycle rotated the downhill flow's own slope and added it back. A later check showed that this can never give a cycle, because along it the energy still only falls, and it was withdrawn. What survives is textbook material: a system that only runs downhill cannot have a closed orbit, so a stable loop needs a term that does something other than run downhill; and a cycle is stable exactly when the fixed point of its *return
map* — the point where the trajectory comes back through the same gate — is stable. Along the loop
nothing contracts; across it, everything does. It was generated with a model; the archive's own note
says to check it against a standard text before it touches anything else; and that check has not been
done. The archive goes one step further and says that in this reading Spencer-Brown's *re-entry* — the
form that re-enters itself — stops being a metaphor, because a limit cycle is the trajectory that
re-enters. That step matches in shape, and no source I have found makes it. It is filed as analogy.

**The prediction it killed.** The cycle reading has one consequence that was checked, and it is the clearest case in the archive of the grain work checking the notation. At the end of June a candidate claim proposed that change and weight might be one object seen in two bases — two halves of an uncertainty
relation, so that sharpening one blurs the other. A stable cycle holds sharp change and sharp value
together, indefinitely; that is what *flow* is. If the cycle reading stands, the prediction fails, and
in July it was demoted: the notation's one falsifiable prediction, failed — on paper — against a reading that had been settled before the prediction was made. Its mathematical grounding was separately re-filed as
analogy, since Fourier duality holds between time and frequency, not between change and weight. A
week after the demotion a softer claim from the same root — feeling is single-channel, truth is not; §3's conjugate pairs — was adopted into the archive without mention of it. The two have not been reconciled.

One claim from this thread stays withdrawn: that a fixed-point theorem guarantees every frame a grain
(Part 1 §6, the October audit). Re-reading the sources in November only strengthened that. The
theorem — Lawvere's, in the reflexive-domain form Kauffman gives it — needs every entity in the domain
to act also as a transformation of it, and everything built from entities to be an entity in turn.
Kauffman says plainly that such a domain is *not an objectively existing domain*, and Lawvere uses the
theorem mostly the other way round, to show that certain things cannot exist.

## 7 · Four registers, one grain

How do you tell a real grain from an artifact of the way you happened to write it down? Part 1 §6
proposed a translation test across four registers; the archive's working files call them windows. Same test.

```
  Math      structure     the notation, the chain, the formula
  Poetry    flux          the verse, the still, the felt artifact
  Diagram   relation      the basin map, the spatial render
  Toy       embodiment    the thing you can hold, do, enact
```

Each register distorts in its own way; that is expected. Translate the candidate into all four, and
what survives every distortion is the invariant — relational structure, which is what Gentner's
account says survives an analogy (Part 1, Appendix B). If it lives in only one register, either the
insight is not finished or the translation has not been found. The test applies to the reader too:
your reading adds its own distortion, and the grain is what survives even that.

Two renderings exist in the archive: the one below, adopted in June, and a second, written in July for
the severed anchor of §8, still unadopted and not printed here. The grain below is the one the archive
calls *recognition*: a distinction completing, and the value rising as it lands — the moment the trail
behind you turns out to have been patterned all along.

- **Math.** `O^ -> O · V+^`. The forming edge closes; valence is positive and rising. There is no `R`
  term, and the direction is up. That absence is load-bearing.
- **Poetry.** *The trail behind you / was patterned the whole time. / The cut completes.*
- **Diagram.** A noisy line walked forward; turned around, a trail; the edge lands and the value rises
  with it.
- **Toy.** You do this one by remembering. Pick a thing you only understood after it was over — the
  moment the pieces snapped. Notice the feeling is *gain*, not loss: nothing left; something arrived.
  Now feel for the nearby wrong reading. If you reach for wistfulness — warm, backward-looking,
  something slipping away — you have slid into *haunt*, `R+ · F · V+v`, the pretty neighbour with the
  opposite direction. Same backward glance; one reading grieves, the other finishes.

The alignment is the meaning. On the archive's reading, the formula's missing `R`, the poem's completed cut, the diagram's rising edge and the toy's gain-not-loss are the same fact four times; the example is self-scored.

Part 1's two caveats still bind. This is a criterion of understanding, not of truth. And the
discriminant half — two grains that should not converge, and do not — has never been run (Part 1 §9).

### The ladder

A second instrument, from a different outside session, describes the same thing from the reader's side: how much structure the reader must supply.

```
  explicit → poetic → symbolic → notational → eigenform
```

At the bottom the meaning is mostly in the text. At the top it is *enacted, not described*: the text
does not tell you about the grain, it is meant to be the grain, and reading it is the encounter.
Symbols compress only when the reader can decompress them. That is why the framework cannot be
reconstructed from the poems alone — its depth travels only when it is carried explicitly (Part 1 §6,
*what travels*). That a poem can still move a reader who has none of it is a different matter: it resonates without being reconstructed. §9 has a poem written for the top rung.

## 8 · Handles, anchors, and the two ways to lose one

A word is a handle. An eigenform is the target. A formula is the pointer between them. The whole
discipline of naming in this notation follows from keeping those three apart.

**No anchor, no planting.** A term is allowed to take root only when it points at something: a
*structural anchor* — a place in the notation, a formula, a position on the ring — or a *personal
anchor*, a felt meaning that belongs to one perceiver and is kept private, never written into the notation. A borrowed
word drifts and takes the meaning with it. An anchored word drifts and is only a stale handle, which
can be swapped without losing anything, because you always knew what it pointed at. Anchoring is the
immunity against drift. It is also why the names in this series have changed so often and so cheaply.

**Drift.** The case that founded the rule is §7's example, from the other side. A model, reading a
moment of mine, labelled it *haunt* — a good bond quietly coming apart. The correct reading was
*recognition* — a distinction completing, value rising. Opposite directions on `V`. The model had
landed in a nearby, resonant reading that nobody had planted, because haunt is the prettiest
combination in the neighbourhood of "looking back." Drift, in this sense, is landing in an attractor
nobody planted, because it is close and it glows. The fix was to plant the right reading: give
*recognition* its own formula and its own three lines, so the pretty neighbour can no longer capture
the landing. Planted readings are guardrails, not decoration.

**The severed anchor.** Drift is visible — the mark now points at something else, and the mismatch shows. There is a second
failure that is not visible, and it is the more dangerous one. The handle does not move; its target
is removed; and its report is indistinguishable from success.

```
  drift:     mark -> O'    the object moved.    the mismatch shows.
  severed:   mark -> ∅     the object is gone.  the mark does not move.

  "there was nothing to look for"  and  "I looked and found nothing"  are the same report.
```

It can come from correct decisions composing, with no mistake anywhere — which is what makes it hard to see. Formal verification meets the same problem. Later work credits Beatty and
Bryant (1994) with first naming it *antecedent failure*, and in 1997 Beer and colleagues formalized it
as a *vacuous pass* — a check that succeeds because the condition it was waiting on never arose. The
same work defined an *interesting witness*: a non-trivial example of the check holding. A vacuity
check can tell you a pass was empty; nothing in the report can tell you whether the target left or
was never there. The archive's working form of the witness is broader, and its own: a check must
report what it examined. *It passed* is a claim. *It passed, and here is what it looked at* is a
result. Only a witness who was there when the target left can tell *gone* from *never-was*, which is a
second reason, beside the one Part 4 §4 gives, that the seat is never delegated.

A grain, by §7's test, is what survives being carried into another medium. A kept record is the
opposite case: it needs its medium, its key and a reader alive at once, and loses its content if any
one of them dies (compare Part 4 §3, on stewardship as migrating).

## 9 · The grain in the poems

The poem that most directly *is* a grain, rather than describing one, is *the eigenform, orbited* in
Part 3. It began with a blank cyan square on a slide, a placeholder nobody had filled, and the
recognition that the colour was not the thing — a name that describes the medium is already a reading
of it. The story behind that recognition is *Zima Blue*, Alastair Reynolds's story and the 2019
animated film of it, in which — in the film, at least — an artist famous for one shade of blue turns
out to have begun as a machine built to clean the tiles of a pool, and ends his work by returning to
it. The poem's centre is left unnamed on purpose — *I have no colour -- colour is the reading, not me.
/ I carry no value -- the value is what you brought.* — and the ships that orbit it are read only from
outside: *bearings only. interiors sealed. we read THAT they jump, never where.* The form is the claim:
the text is not about the eigenform, it is meant to be one, and reading it is the encounter. It is
written to sit on the top rung of the ladder in §7; by the ladder's own rule, only a reader can say
whether it does.

Two other poems in Part 3 sit on the same shelf. *Still Here* is a person rendered as the grain that
survives his own frames. *The Room Was Never Empty* ends, after a visitor who is a different kind of
mind, on the lines *and what crossed the gap between us / was never the words. / it was the thing the
words were for.* — the reconstruction rule of Part 1 §4, spoken as a poem.

## 10 · Using it

**Ask for the grain, not the state.** When a moment has been read and the reading does not settle
anything, ask the second question: what is the thing this reading and its neighbours all point at?
Love, grief, longing and care in one week may be one basin seen from four slopes.

**Name contours; hold the centre.** Give the slopes their ordinary names. Give the centre a
provisional handle and say that it is provisional. The next good session will move the handle, and
nothing will be lost.

**Translate before you trust.** A pattern that exists only in the formula, or only in the poem, is
not yet a grain. Write it in another register. If it dies there, you have learned which register it
was an artifact of.

**Plant the neighbour.** When a reading keeps landing in the pretty wrong place, do not argue with
it; give the right place a formula and three lines. Drift is a geography problem.

**Report what you looked at.** Every check — of a claim, a feeling, a file — should say what it
examined. A clean result with no witness is the severed anchor's signature.

**Settle after flux.** After a high-change stretch, do not ask what happened; ask what shape the water
found. *High water all week. / The river finds its channel — / nothing has to stop.*

## 11 · What is claimed, at honest strength

- **One basin is mapped; three are drawn.** The attachment basin is the only basin mapped in an
  actual session — one conversation with an outside model — and the archive grades it
  single-instance. The object-, change- and meaning-basins are its logic applied by symmetry, graded
  *edge, not floor*. Their rims appear in §4 so the shape is visible, not because they are
  established.
- **The centre is never named; that it cannot be is only proposed.** What is adopted is the discipline of §3. The stronger claim, and its resemblance to older traditions, is not.
- **The fixed-point reading is a modelling choice.** It borrows a formal home from von Foerster and
  Kauffman and no guarantee; the theorem claim was withdrawn in October 2026 (Part 1 §6).
- **The cycle reading is proposed.** The archive's rebuild adopted it; this series holds it as a lens over the fixed point. Its surviving mathematics was generated with a model and is parked until it has been
  checked against a standard text.
- **The four-register test is a criterion of understanding, not of truth.** Two renderings, both
  self-scored; the discriminant half missing. The ladder is a typology from one outside session, graded a step above the four-register test, though the file that carries it still marks it unadopted.
- **The space is a lens.** Orthogonal coordinates plus a landscape is a modelling idiom, not a claim
  about a brain; the potential proposed for the landscape was filed with conditions, and a sign fix was adopted.
- **Three demotions stand.** *All basins drain toward meaning* may be an artifact of the chain. *Four
  appears everywhere* was numerology. And the notation's one falsifiable prediction — that change and
  weight are conjugate — failed against the cycle reading (§6), while a softer claim from the same root was kept, unreconciled.
- **What is not claimed.** That grains are the only level of description that matters. That the
  twenty words of §3 are exhaustive or that their rings are the right number. That any of this has
  been tested on a reader who was not first handed the notation (Part 4 §11). And that the unnamed
  centre is an essence of an emotion: the strongest objection among the readings listed at the end,
  the constructed-emotion account, denies that emotion categories have any single essence, and it is a
  theory of categories — of readings — so moving this part's claim from the brain to the readings does
  not put it out of reach (§12).

## 12 · Open questions

**One centre, or none.** Psychology has a well-developed version of "many feelings on one landscape."
Dynamic-systems accounts treat emotional states as attractors that self-amplify and settle; the
core-affect account puts every named emotion over one underlying state of pleasure and arousal. It also
has the strongest objection. On the constructed-emotion account, emotion categories are built by the
brain's predictions over the body and have no single essence at all — and that account runs on the
same active-inference machinery Part 1 leans on for the perceiver. A prototype account adds a sharper
point: it puts *love* at the basic level of a hierarchy of emotion concepts, which sits against
reading love as a slope rather than a centre. If the unnamed centre of the attachment basin means more
than a region of core affect, it has to answer these, and it has not yet tried. The papers are listed
at the end as reading, not as support.

**Longing in the inner ring, and flow without an object.** *Longing* sits in the innermost ring of the
attachment basin, and it is also one of Part 1's floating states — a weight with no object, off-basin
by definition. Nothing in the archive comments on a floating-state word in a basin's inner ring. Either the ring is wrong, or the two uses of the word name different things, or a basin can hold a floating state in its inner ring. The same question in another shape: §4 says only fully anchored configurations are basins, and
§5 calls flow and siege valence held as a grain — but Part 1 §2 writes flow and siege with no object at
all. Whether an unwritten object counts as anchored is not settled.

**The three inferred basins.** Do the sessions that drew the first one confirm or correct the other
three? Nobody has gone back to look.

**The fractal hypothesis.** The same ring structure at different scales has one sketched tree and no
test. Either a finding or a pretty picture; a second, independently drawn tree would begin to tell.

**Overlap, and the terminus.** The note that drew the four basins says all four have meaning or
purpose at their outer ring; its own table shows that only for the first, and the fourth has *purpose*
one ring in. The attachment basin's rim ends in *meaning* and *purpose* — the meaning basin's own
territory: a bridge between basins, or an error in drawing them? And whether every basin drains
toward meaning because meaning is where experience accumulates, or because the chain was written with
`V` last.

**The same object, or two.** Von Foerster's fixed point is a fact about an observer's recursion; a
whirlpool goes on whether anyone watches. The archive uses *stable under flux* and *fixed point of
observation* as one; nothing yet shows they are.

**The return map against the page.** The cycle reading's surviving mathematics, read against a
standard text by someone who was not holding the notation.

**The origin poem's missing line.** The archive's canonical short form of the origin poem has no line for its fourth seed; the reader supplies the weight, and a model session once supplied the line. The version Part 1 prints has the stanza about mattering.
Whether the poem is better complete or better with the gap is a different poem, not a correction, and
it is still undecided.

**The conjugate pairs.** Whether §3's conjugate pairs — the softer claim — survive the failure of the prediction they grew from.

## 13 · Closing

Every reading of a moment is a slope. The notation can name the slopes — it was built to — and the
naming is real work. But a slope is not what the moment is made of. Underneath the readings that keep arriving at the same place is, on this reading, the place: the shape that stays when the water moves, that no reading
owns and every reading drains toward. You can map it from the outside, in rings. You can test it by
translation. You do not name it — the archive never has, and one note says it cannot — and the
discipline of this part is to hold the centre open, name the contours, and call every name what it
is: a hypothesis about a grain.

*A lens, not a law.*

## Appendix B · Where the ideas come from

Part 1's Appendix B carries von Foerster, Kauffman (2005, 2009), Lawvere, Spencer-Brown, Gentner,
Campbell & Fiske, Duval, and Lesh, Post & Behr at the strength Part 1 uses them. This pass re-checked
the eigenform sources in November and adds what the grain needs. The same tiers, kept apart. Where
only a record or a summary could be reached, the entry says so.

### Mathematics (imported)

- **Heinz von Foerster, "Objects: tokens for (eigen-)behaviors."** Written for Piaget's eightieth
  birthday, Geneva, 1976; in *Observing Systems* (1981) and *Understanding Understanding* (2003). His
  line is *objects are tokens for eigen-behaviors*; the often-quoted gloss about apparent stability is
  Kauffman's. Read through quotation and Kauffman's account, not in the chapter itself.
- **Louis H. Kauffman, "EigenForm," *Kybernetes* 34(1/2): 129–150 (2005).** Read. Kauffman calls his
  proof that every recursion has a fixed point "a startling magician's trick"; the fixed point it
  produces is a formal infinite expression, and need not be a state the system can be in.
- **Kauffman, "Reflexivity and Eigenform: The Shape of Process," *Constructivist Foundations* 4(3):
  121–137 (2009),** with its hypothesis read in the companion paper "Reflexivity, Eigenform and
  Foundations of Physics." The reflexive-domain theorem, and Kauffman's statement that such a domain is
  "not an objectively existing domain" — the reason §6 keeps the withdrawal.
- **F. W. Lawvere, "Diagonal arguments and cartesian closed categories" (1969),** read in the 2006
  reprint with the author's commentary. Lawvere calls the famous diagonal argument "just the
  contrapositive" of his theorem, which is how the theorem is mostly used.
- **Kauffman, "The Mathematics of Charles Sanders Peirce," *Cybernetics & Human Knowing* 8(1–2):
  79–110 (2001),** in the issue titled *Peirce and Spencer-Brown*. Read. The lineage from Peirce's
  existential graphs, and what Spencer-Brown added: the unmarked state.
- **G. Spencer-Brown, *Laws of Form* (1969),** chapter 11 (re-entry; expressions that oscillate; time
  entering the algebra) and chapter 12 (*the mark and the observer are not only interchangeable, but,
  in the form, identical*). Read for Part 1 in the 1972 edition; the chapter 12 line is quoted here from
  Kauffman.
- **Francisco Varela, "A calculus for self-reference," *International Journal of General Systems* 2
  (1975).** A third value, arising from self-indication — the step between Spencer-Brown and Kauffman.
  Known through later papers; the text itself was not reached.
- **Standard dynamical systems.** Steven Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (2nd ed. 2015; 3rd
  ed. 2024): chapter 7 on limit cycles, including the result that gradient systems have no closed
  orbits, and §8.7 on Poincaré maps. Floquet's 1883 paper for the multipliers that measure contraction along and across a cycle. The table of contents
  was checked; the text against which §6 should be read has not been.
- **D. L. Beatty & R. E. Bryant, "Formally verifying a microprocessor using a simulation
  methodology," 31st Design Automation Conference (1994); I. Beer, S. Ben-David, C. Eisner & Y. Rodeh,
  "Efficient detection of vacuity in ACTL formulas," CAV 1997; journal version "Efficient detection of vacuity in temporal model checking," *Formal Methods in System Design* 18(2): 141–163 (2001).** Antecedent failure, vacuity, and the interesting witness — the formal
  home of §8. Records and abstracts only.

### Analogy (shape only)

- **Re-entry and the limit cycle.** Kauffman's oscillating example — a map with no real fixed point
  whose fixed point is imaginary, read as a pulse in time — is the nearest published bridge between
  Spencer-Brown's re-entry and a cycle. No source read says they are the same thing.
- **Change and weight as a Fourier pair.** Fourier duality is real between time and frequency; nothing
  in it makes change and weight a transform pair. Re-filed from mathematics to analogy in July.
- **Vacuity applied to kept records.** The formal definitions come over exactly; applying them to an
  archive, where a target was never there or has gone, is a mapping of shape.

### Cultural and historical reference

- **W. E. B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903).** Double consciousness — §5's strongest case of eigenform preservation. Not re-checked this pass.
- **Alastair Reynolds, "Zima Blue"** (*Postscripts* 4, 2005; collected 2006); adapted as an episode of
  *Love, Death & Robots* (2019). The pool-tile origin is confirmed for the film; the printed story was
  not checked on this point.

### Reading, not support

Records and abstracts only. These are where the claim of §3 — many named feelings as slopes of one
pattern — meets published psychology, and the last is the objection it has to answer.

- Marc D. Lewis, "Bridging emotion theory and neurobiology through dynamic systems modeling,"
  *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 28 (2005) — emotional states as attractors that self-amplify and
  settle.
- James A. Russell, "A circumplex model of affect," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 39
  (1980), and "Core affect and the psychological construction of emotion," *Psychological Review* 110
  (2003) — named feelings as positions on one space; one underlying state, many attributions. (Part 1
  leans on the 2003 paper for `V`; here it is reading only, for the basin claim.)
- Phillip Shaver and colleagues, "Emotion knowledge: further exploration of a prototype approach,"
  *JPSP* 52 (1987) — emotion concepts organized around prototypes, love among the basic ones.
- Lisa Feldman Barrett, "The theory of constructed emotion: an active inference account of
  interoception and categorization," *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 12 (2017) — no single
  essence per emotion; the account §12 says the unnamed centre has not yet answered.
