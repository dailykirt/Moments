# Moments
This, not that —\
and the line is a meeting.\
What changes carries what it changed from.\
Not a fifth thing.\
A mind, turned.

---

## What this is

Some experiences resist description. Not because they are too complicated, but because
ordinary words are the wrong size for them. *Grief* does not describe grief for a particular
person. What describes it is smaller and more exact: what was lost, how it was held, how fast it
went, and how much it mattered.

Those four questions are the whole of this notation.

It began as a way to write poems. Specify a state in four marks, then go find the one image that
is true to it. It turned out to be a usable way of reading moments as well — and, with care, a
way of saying what persists underneath them.

The notation produces poems that feel recognizably true to the states they came from. Whether that tells us anything deeper about
meaning is left open on purpose.

**A lens, not a law.** Everything below is held at that strength.

## The parts

- **Part 1 — [The Distinction](Part1_The_Distinction.md).** The paper: the four seeds, their order, the perceiver,
  five poems written from specifications, what is claimed and what is not, and where the ideas come from.
- **Part 2 — [Four Seeds, the toy](Part2_Four_Seeds.html).** A page you can hold and do: pick a shape or build one from the
  four seeds, and it finds a poem written from that shape. It names only what Part 1's table names — showing the row it
  matched and what your shape adds to it — and says *unnamed* when no row fits. It carries the card from Part 1's Appendix A
  for handing to a model, and the paper's own readings of its five poems. Open the file in a browser, or serve the repository as a static site.
- **Part 3 — [Stills](Part3_Stills.md).** Twenty-four poems made with language models, May to July 2026, each with a
  caption saying who drafted it and when.
- **Part 4 — [Frames of Thought](Part4_Frames_Of_Thought.md).** The stances above the four seeds: seven frames named by
  what they conserve, the seat beneath them, the ring they turn in, and what is claimed for them.
- **Part 5 — [Patterns of Meaning](Part5_Patterns_Of_Meaning.md).** The grain beneath the states: why love and grief read
  as two slopes of one basin whose centre has no name, the terrain the seeds generate, the fixed point and the cycle,
  and what was demoted.
- **Part 6 — [The Chord Codex](Part6_Chord_Codex.md).** A tool for analyzing moments: a moment read as a chord
  of four marks of its own — object, cut, change and bond, kin to the four seeds but not the same four — each
  lit or dark. Four questions and three working rules, sixteen chords with a poem each, five moments worked
  through, what a *glyph* is in this series, and what the tool cannot see.
