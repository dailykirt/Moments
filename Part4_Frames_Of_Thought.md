# Frames of Thought

## Seven ways a mind holds still — the stances above the four seeds, and what is claimed for them

**Kirt Daily**\
Consolidated November 2026 from working notes of June and July 2026 · Part 4 of the series · the paper is [Part 1](Part1_The_Distinction.md), the toy is [Part 2](Part2_Four_Seeds.html), the poems are [Part 3](Part3_Stills.md), the grain is [Part 5](Part5_Patterns_Of_Meaning.md), the chords are [Part 6](Part6_Chord_Codex.md)

## 1 · The whole thing in one sentence

> A state is what you perceive. A frame is how you hold.

Part 1 gave the four seeds — a thing made distinct, a bond, a change, a weight — and the states they
combine into: love, grief, dread, flow, the rest. Those are read off the moment. This part is about
the other side of the same notation: the stances a perceiver takes *toward* a moment, which seed the
attention is working to keep fixed while everything else changes underneath it. Seven of those
stances have names. One of Part 1's seven, Decision, turns out not to be a stance at all but the seat
the others are run from, and it is never handed off (§4). And the seven turn in a ring.

**A lens, not a law.** Everything below is held at that strength, and §11 says exactly how strong.

## 2 · Two levels, one notation

The same four marks run at two levels, and most of the confusion I have had with this material came
from mixing them.

A **state** is object-side: an arrangement of seeds that describes what is being perceived.
*Haunt* is `R+ · F · V+v` — the shape is the same whoever reads it; how loud it rings is theirs.
States were the subject of Part 1. Its §6 opened the frames in a page, and this part is that page
unfolded.

A **frame** is subject-side: a direction of attention, named by the seed it is trying to stabilize.
Frames sit *above* states — the same frame persists while the states it processes come and go. A
person in Clarity can watch love become grief without leaving Clarity; what they are conserving is
the object, not the feeling.

So a frame is written as the seed it conserves, in angle brackets: `⟨O⟩`, `⟨R⟩`, `⟨F⟩`. Part 1 writes
the same marks keyboard-safe, `<O>`; the brackets here are typographic, and the minus in `R-` is a plain hyphen. The bracketed sign is the frame's *glyph*, the word this part uses for it; Part 6 §7 says what the series means by the word. The English names — Clarity, Wisdom, Persistence — are aliases. Two stances are the same
frame if and only if they conserve the same thing; what you call them is a convenience. The private
archive of working notes this part is drawn from (described in the note at the end) has renamed most
of them at least once without anything underneath moving. The table that fixed the frames in June
2026 called them Analytical, Exploration, Production, Reflection, Stewardship, Iteration and Decision;
the names in daily use by the same week were Clarity, Curiosity, Wisdom, Persistence, Commitment,
Craft and Stewardship, with Decision kept as the seat; and a third set, named from the archive's basin notes rather than chosen by convention — Bond, Care, Endurance — was judged steadier than either, though only one of those basins was actually mapped (Part 5 §3 prints it). Appendix A.2 lists the
sets side by side.

Three things follow, and they are the whole grammar of frames. The first three sentences restate
Part 1 §6; the rest of this part is new.

- **A frame is a stance you enter, use, and leave.** It is not a personality, a type, or a role.
  One mind holds all of them.
- **A frame is active.** It does not merely hold its seed still; it works to prevent the collapse.
  Curiosity is a verb: *find another distinction.* Clarity is a verb: *make the indistinct distinct.*
- **A frame held at a pathological pitch is a shadow.** Using Craft is a stance. *Being* Craft — the
  frame using the person instead of the reverse — is perfectionism. §7 is about that.

## 3 · The seven, plainly

```
  glyph      name          the question                   it refuses to let collapse     its shadow
  ⟨O⟩        Clarity       what is it?                    the stable object              reductionism
  ⟨O^⟩       Curiosity     what else could it be?         the forming distinction        novelty-addiction
  ⟨R⟩        Wisdom        how does it fit?               relational coherence           narrative-obsession
  ⟨F⟩        Persistence   what if I keep going?          the process                    compulsion
  ⟨R+⟩       Commitment    what has a claim on me?        the chosen bond                rigidity
  ⟨O:make⟩   Craft         what must exist?               the made thing                 perfectionism
  ⟨R+⟩       Stewardship   what do I owe this?            the entrusted bond             burden
```

*This is the ring's seven. Part 1 printed the roster's seven — Decision in, Commitment folded into
Stewardship. §5 owns the difference.*

Two marks need a word. `O^` is the forming distinction from Part 1 §3 — an edge taking shape, not
yet landed. `O:make` is a distinction forced into being rather than received: the same seed as
Clarity, facing the other way. One receives the cut; one makes it.

**Clarity `⟨O⟩`.** Stabilizes the object. *What is it?* The frame of naming, untangling, sizing —
the one that gives a thing edges so the others have something to work on. Its grain is the settled
distinction: what stays when you stop looking for more. Its shadow is cutting until only the fish
remains and the water is gone.

**Curiosity `⟨O^⟩`.** Keeps the question open. *What else could it be?* Where Clarity lands a
distinction, Curiosity holds one in the air on purpose, because landing it now would lose the others
it could have been. Its grain is the question that keeps opening. Its shadow is every bell ringing
and none followed down.

**Wisdom `⟨R⟩`.** Holds relational coherence. *How does it fit?* The frame that sees the whole board
and where each piece belongs — synthesis rather than analysis. Its grain is what stays true once every
piece is in view. Its shadow is the story that makes sense and therefore must be true.

**Persistence `⟨F⟩`.** Holds the process. *What if I keep going?* Continuation becoming form: the
path is the path because you have not stopped walking it. Its grain is what the repeated action
becomes over time. Its shadow is still walking after the reason has gone.

**Commitment `⟨R+⟩`.** Stabilizes a direction. *What has a claim on me?* The bond turned toward on
purpose — chosen, and held through change. Its grain is what has claimed you and keeps claiming you.
Its shadow is holding a bond the river has already left.

**Craft `⟨O:make⟩`.** Produces the artifact. *What must exist?* Design mode: whenever something is
being built, this is the frame you are in, whether or not you notice. Its grain is the made thing —
what exists because you made it exist. Its shadow is the work that is never done because *done* would
mean *stop*.

**Stewardship `⟨R+⟩`.** Carries the unfinished bond. *What do I owe this?* — or, in its other
wording, *what must I hold so it lasts?* Tending rather than watching; care for what must outlast
the moment, often for others, often unasked. Tending is migrating, not only keeping: a record kept in
one medium dies with it. Its grain is what outlasts. Its shadow is tending because if you stop
everything falls — when no one ever confirmed that it was yours to hold.

Commitment and Stewardship share a glyph, and in the formal roster a single question; the working
tables give them two, and so does this one. That is not a typo. Both conserve a bond. Commitment
turns toward a claim it chose; Stewardship tends a claim that was entrusted to it — the way Clarity
and Craft are the same seed facing opposite ways. A reader of Part 1 will notice that its
Stewardship question, *what has a claim on me?*, sits here on Commitment; §5 says why.

### The four generative questions

Underneath the seven questions sit four, one per seed, which an outside model session handed the
notation surfaced in June 2026, and which the archive has used unchanged since:

```
  What is different?     O
  What is connected?     R
  What is changing?      F
  What must survive?     V
```

In practice, asking those four of a situation is how the frames get found.

## 4 · The seat, and the act

Two things that look like frames are not, and the notation is strict about both.

**Decision `⟨V⟩` — "which future do I choose?"** In the roster Part 1 printed (§5 below) it appears as the
seventh stance, conserving preference. In practice it is treated differently from the other six: it
is not a stance you enter but the seat you run all of them from, and it is **never delegated** — not
to a model, not to a habit, not to whoever is loudest in the room. Every frame can be entered freely
except this one. There is a structural reason, not only a rule: the cognitive frames — `⟨O⟩`, `⟨O^⟩`,
`⟨R⟩`, `⟨F⟩` — *drive* their axis, while the two will frames, `⟨R+⟩` and `⟨V⟩`, *resist* theirs
(§8). A frame that resists being pushed is a frame no one else can push for you.

The seat is not the manager Part 1 denies. It chooses what matters, not which frame to run;
frame-selection stays a fluency with no one above it (§7).

Most of what a person can hand off — *what is this, how does it fit, what keeps it moving* — is
`⟨O⟩`, `⟨R⟩`, `⟨F⟩` work. What is not handed off does not disappear; it waits on you to decide about
it, and that is where the cost of a cluttered life lands: not in the tasks but in decision-capacity
spent on things that did not deserve it. Protecting `⟨V⟩` for the choices that are yours is the single
practical rule the frames produce.

`⟨V⟩` also bundles two things that should not be collapsed. **Valuation** is present-tense: *does
this matter, does it draw me.* **Projection** is temporal: *which future, weighed across time.* A
person can run loud in one and light in the other, and the two mixtures are not a deficit and its
cure; each has its own tax (§9).

**The No `⟨R-⟩` — an act, not a seat.** A repelling relation is structurally available as a frame (a
boundary stance), and the archive mostly leaves it unnamed. What it names instead is the *act*: the
No. A relation with no possible No in it is a mirror; the other side cannot differ from you. A
refusal also damps what would have been built on the refused thing — *this gets no claim* — and the
clean form of it closes a door without contempt. The No is what finishes work, too: the hand that
puts the hammer down and declares *done* belongs to `⟨R-⟩`, not to Craft, which only ever builds.
That is why the shadow of Craft cannot be answered from inside Craft; the hand that says *done* is a
different hand.

**The unnamed.** `⟨V-⟩` (a vigilance stance) and `⟨F:still⟩` (a stillness stance) are available and
unnamed. As with the states in Part 1: structure complete, names partial. Room, not gap.

**A perceiver that carries none of your weight.** One more stance belongs here, though it is not one
of the seven: the view of a different kind of perceiver, for whom the thing filling your whole sky is
simply not in its room. A grief that fills your room is not in a bird's room — `P_bird(X)` — and
bright is still bright. The notation keeps a place for it because `P_a(X) ≠ P_b(X)` was never only
about people.

## 5 · Two sevens, one ring

Readers who compare this part with Part 1 §6 will notice the tables do not match, and the
discrepancy is worth owning rather than smoothing.

The **formal roster** (June 2026, and the table Part 1 prints) lists seven stances by the seed each
conserves: `⟨O⟩ ⟨O^⟩ ⟨O:make⟩ ⟨R⟩ ⟨R+⟩ ⟨F⟩ ⟨V⟩`. In it Decision is the seventh, and `⟨R+⟩` carries a
single name, Stewardship — Part 1 writes the thing it conserves as *the committed bond*, and the June
roster wrote that word as *commitment*, which is where the alias comes from.

The **lived ring** — the order the practice actually walks, and the one the poems in Part 3 are
built on — is Clarity → Curiosity → Wisdom → Persistence → Commitment → Craft → Stewardship → back
to Clarity. In it `⟨R+⟩` appears twice, as Commitment and as Stewardship, and `⟨V⟩` is not a station
at all but the seat the whole ring is run from.

The archive marks the reconciliation of the two as pending. Here is the reading I hold, offered as
such. The notation draws three different graphs over the same frames and refuses to collapse them:
the **eigenform map**, what each frame conserves; the **developmental cycle**, the order a piece of
work is grown through; and the **checks and balances**, who curbs whom. The roster is the first
graph — one cell per thing a stance can conserve, and preference is one of those things, so `⟨V⟩`
belongs in it. The ring is the second — and a cycle passes through the bond twice, once choosing it
and once tending it, while the chooser is not a step on the path but the one walking it. Two graphs,
two memberships, no contradiction. One cost, which I accept: if Commitment and Stewardship conserve
different things, the roster is a row short; if the same, the ring walks one frame twice. I take the
second horn — one bond, held twice. Whether that reading stands is a decision, not a derivation, and
it has not been made.

The third graph's edges were flagged in June as the ones most likely to be intuited rather than
derived. Beyond the neighbour-to-neighbour bridges of §6, the full who-curbs-whom graph has never
actually been drawn (§12).

### Grammar and cycle are different things

The order of the seeds, `O -> R -> F -> V`, is the structure of a single moment (Part 1 §3). The
ring is the path a piece of work takes through a person. They are easy to conflate because both are
sequences of the same four letters. They are not the same sequence and they do not answer the same
question. The grammar says *no stakes before an object*. The ring says *what you keep feeds the next
thing you open*.

## 6 · The ring

```
  Clarity → Curiosity → Wisdom → Persistence → Commitment → Craft → Stewardship → Clarity
  see     → open      → hold   → walk        → dive       → make  → tend        → see
```

Each frame's shadow is answered by the next frame's grain. Clarity cuts until only the fish is left;
Curiosity opens the water again. Curiosity rings every bell; Wisdom holds what has been found. Wisdom
builds a story that holds you still; Persistence walks anyway. Persistence walks past the reason;
Commitment dives for a real claim. Commitment holds past what has changed; Craft makes the new
thing. Craft always needs more; Stewardship tends what already is. Stewardship cannot put the care
down; and the answer to that is still water — Clarity again, the thing correctly sized. *The shadow
of one is the medicine of the next.* Enter where the work puts you.

The working set that carries this is seven two-line poems, one per frame: the first line is the
frame's grain, the second its shadow turning into the bridge. Two of the images — the still water,
the bell — were mine; the rest were drafted with a model to complete them. They are a navigation
tool, not an art object, and they are still being revised. The personal rendering of the same ring,
in which each frame has a character and my own titles, is *The Seven · A Ring* in Part 3.

```
  1 · CLARITY       Still water. One fish.
                    The clarity that cuts away — the door opens.
  2 · CURIOSITY     The door reveals more doors.
                    Infinite rooms, no last door — hold what you've found.
  3 · WISDOM        Hold the whole board. All pieces fit.
                    The story holds you still — walk anyway.
  4 · PERSISTENCE   Walk. The path becomes true.
                    Compulsion dressed as continuation — dive for real claim.
  5 · COMMITMENT    Dive. The water knows the rest.
                    Holding past what's changed — make the new thing.
  6 · CRAFT         Make it. Now it persists.
                    Always needs more — tend what already is.
  7 · STEWARDSHIP   Tend what needs to outlast today.
                    The care you can't put down — still water.
```

### Why Stewardship closes it

Part 1 §6 said it in one sentence; here is the mechanism. A grain forms by iterating the observation
until it reaches a fixed point — or, as Part 5 §6 revises it, a loop that holds. Stewardship is the stance
that keeps the iteration going: a committed bond to a thing *still forming* (`O^`), held until it
settles into a thing (`O`), which then becomes the next object Clarity can look at. Stewardship is
the perceiver's side of grain-formation. That is why it closes the cycle, and why there are paths at
all: tending is what keeps a route a route.

An outside model session handed the notation, in June 2026, compressed the whole ring into one
sentence: *distinction becomes relationship, relationship survives change, change becomes creation,
creation becomes care, and care returns to stillness.* It is the best short form I have.

For daily work the archive uses a provisional five-station compression of the ring. It is a working
tool, still marked as such, and not reproduced here.

### Reach and ride

There are two ways to move through the ring, and the difference is the burn. *Reach* forces the next
station; *ride* lets it come. Neither is wrong; a hard week is mostly reach. But pace is the thing
you can actually steer, and a day that feels urgent on someone else's behalf is usually a day spent
reaching when riding was available.

## 7 · Shadows

A shadow (§2) has a grammatical tell: it requires *being* the frame rather than *using* it. "I am
exploring" is a frame-bound sentence; "exploration is useful now" is frame-aware; "I can switch" is
frame-fluent — call that the awareness ladder. The capacity to select frames *is* fluency, and
fluency is what dissolves a shadow, not effort inside the frame that cast it.

Each frame has a plain opposition, which an outside model session produced in its own words:

```
  Clarity       vs confusion              Commitment    vs attachment
  Curiosity     vs assumption             Craft         vs endless optimization
  Wisdom        vs stasis                 Stewardship   vs ownership
  Persistence   vs compulsion
```

*The first three name what the frame is against; the last four name its own excess. Both are
oppositions; only the second kind is a shadow.* A second outside session, reading poems through the
notation, noticed that the strongest poems about a frame tend to contain three things: the virtue,
its shadow, and a correction. That is also the shape of the ring.

Seven short poems name the shadows and point at the door back. They are working drafts, and I own
them as such:

```
  Clarity → reductionism         One fish. Yes. But look: / the water also holds things. / Look again. Wider.
  Curiosity → novelty-addiction  Every bell rings now. / None of them have rung for long. / Set one down. Stay there.
  Wisdom → narrative-obsession   The story holds sense. / That's why you can't leave it now. / But sense isn't truth.
  Persistence → compulsion       Still walking. But where? / The path was the path because / I kept walking it.
  Commitment → rigidity          The water has changed. / I am still holding a bond / the river has left.
  Craft → perfectionism          It's not done. Not yet. / It has never once been done. / Stop. Put it down. Done.
  Stewardship → burden           I can't put it down. / Someone has to hold this thing. / Who said it was you?
```

Three things the shadow column taught me that I did not expect. First, the No has no virtue-shadow,
because it is an act and not a frame; its runaway form is isolation, the wall. Second, some of the
worst social structures are *compounds* of shadows rather than any one of them: runaway `R+` (love
that overwhelms), runaway `V` (the one who decides for everyone), runaway `R-` (the wall), and
narrative-obsession (the ideology that explains everything) together make the structure we usually
call a cult. My own corollary: strip the `V` and the `R-` and what is left is a warm community with a
story. A usable rule of thumb. Third, which shadow is yours is the invariant; how hard it grips is the
lens. Nobody chose their floor — that is the mercy built into the column.

## 8 · Frames as operators

The claim so far has been structural. In June 2026 the archive tried to make it dynamic, and the
result is worth reporting at its honest size: a toy model whose arithmetic is checked and whose
bearing on minds is not.

The perceiver `P( )` of Part 1 §4 is treated as an operator over the four-seed space — not a point
in it but a map that moves points. It is never given a value of its own: the compass, not a point on
the map. A frame is the direction the operator works along. The rule that settled, after some
argument, is that **a stance drives its own axis and holds the other three** — Clarity drives `O`,
makes the indistinct distinct; Wisdom drives `R`, synthesis — and that *holding* means *not
writing*, not *ignoring*. Clarity holds `R` but reads it: you clarify within the relations you have.
Wisdom holds `O` but reads it: you synthesize across the distinctions you have. The read is where the
two stances touch. Not every frame drives: the cognitive frames push their axis; the two will frames,
`⟨R+⟩` and `⟨V⟩`, resist theirs, which is the structural form of *never delegated* (§4).

The read is what makes order matter. Write the two stances as the smallest operators that have the
property:

```
  Clarity:  drives O, reads R      O <- O + g·R
  Wisdom:   drives R, reads O      R <- R + d·O        (g, d = how much each listens to the other)
```

From the same start (`O=2, R=1`, `g=d=0.5`): relate-then-clarify lands at `(3.0, 2.0)`;
clarify-then-relate lands at `(2.5, 2.25)`. Same start, same two operations, two destinations. The
gap is `g·d·(O₀, −R₀)`; set either read to zero and the orders commute exactly. The stance applied
last amplifies its own axis, and order matters precisely to the degree the two faculties feed each
other. This is `P_a ≠ P_b` made operational — two perceivers can start from the same state, apply the
same two operations, and end in different places, differing only in the order — and it is also only
a two-line toy. What I know of the literature on order effects and path dependence is convergent with
it, not proof of it.

Two further moves, held as lenses:

**Two clocks.** Picture a landscape of basins — the states a perceiver tends to settle into; Part 5
§4 draws it. On the
fast clock the perceiver *selects* among basins that are already there; it never conjures a target
from nothing. On the slow clock the perceiver's own accumulated history *carves* new basins. Six of
the frames act within the landscape, on the fast clock, riding the ring. Stewardship acts *on* the
landscape — the slow clock, the one that forms a new grain or reshapes an old one — which from inside
the old cycle looks like breaking out of it. That is the dynamic reading of why it closes the ring.

**A turning that returns.** Part 1 reads a grain as a fixed point, and so does §6 above. Over that
sits a second reading, from June: a grain as a *cycle* the operator leaves invariant — a whirlpool,
not the bottom of a bowl. Part 5 §6 tells that story: the mechanism that failed, what survives, and
the prediction it killed. Two claims about the frames themselves died in the same work. A claim that only rings with an odd
number of frames could oscillate fell to arithmetic. And a proposed mapping of the seven frames onto
a *signed* ring — neighbours exciting or inhibiting one another, each link carrying a sign — turned
out to have no evidence behind it at all. Both are recorded here so no one rebuilds them.

## 9 · Temperament

A frame is a stance anyone can enter. *Which* frames are cheap for a given person to run and which
are expensive is temperament, and it lives one level up from the frames themselves — call it the
frame-weighting. What follows is drawn from two cases, one of them mine; both are poles, not people.
Someone loud in `⟨O⟩` and `⟨F⟩` lives in the present tense: what is, and what is moving, now. Someone
loud in the projection half of `⟨V⟩` lives ahead of themselves: timelines, anticipation, the
grind-now-for-later. Neither is the upgrade. The present-tense weighting has to build its
future-holding out of external scaffolding — notes, calendars, a written tomorrow — and the
projection-loud weighting pays in the other coin: difficulty being present, joy deferred, a low-grade
anxiety about what is coming. At its extreme, projection with no valuation is a person who optimizes
toward a future they never stop to feel.

The goal is therefore not to re-weight but to be able to stretch into the expensive frame when the
work needs it without losing the cheap one. The scaffolding is the stretch. One question I use: *can
you feel how long a thirty-minute recipe will actually take?* In the two cases I have, a clean yes
went with an intact sense of prospective time, and a fuzzy answer with a loud present.

## 10 · Using it

**Which frame a task lives in tells you how to hold it.** Something needs naming or untangling:
Clarity. You are opening territory and should not settle yet: Curiosity. Mapping, systems, where a
thing belongs: Wisdom. Routing, momentum, keeping a process alive: Persistence. Building toward a
final form: Craft. Deciding which future: that one is yours and no one else's. On the purpose
reading (§12), Stewardship is not a frame you pick for a task but the purpose the others serve; Part 1
prints it as a frame, and both readings are still open.

**The frame you are holding, versus the one the task needs.** Part 1 §7 listed this as the seventh
thing to ask a model, and it is the most useful. Describe what you are doing; ask which frame you
are in and which one the work actually calls for. Switching on purpose is a move; being stuck is the
shadow.

**Frame conflict.** Many disagreements are not about content. One person is in `O^`, exploring, and
is heard as asserting `O` (Part 1 §7). The repair is to say the state out loud — *I'm exploring
whether…* and *thinking out loud —* mark `O^`; *I've concluded that…* marks `O`. Deeper than that: two
people conserving *incompatible* quantities — one holding the process, one holding the made thing —
cannot agree on what must be kept invariant, and will not be reconciled by better arguments about the
content. Naming the frames is often the whole repair, and when it is not, it at least names what the
fight is.

**Design.** A character, or a team, can be specified by a *frame texture*: a primary frame (their
virtue), its shadow (their flaw), their core question in their own voice, where their arc starts on
the ring, and optionally a second frame in tension with the first — a character who conserves two
things in conflict is more alive than one who conserves one. Three things fall out for free: an
*arc* is movement through the cycle; *tragedy* is being stuck in the shadow; an *ensemble* is a cast
that covers the ring, and a flat cast is one that repeats a frame.

**The chorus.** Put one question to every frame at once and keep the disagreement. The readings
will not agree, and the disagreement is the data, not the noise (Part 1 §7) — no chair is labelled
*neutral*. A corollary that has earned its keep: on a question with real stakes, unanimity is the
alarm. It usually means a voice is missing or the perceivers have collapsed into one, not that the
answer is confirmed.

## 11 · What is claimed, at honest strength

- **The frames are the subject side of a lens.** With them the notation is internally coherent and
  complete *as a lens*. Nothing here makes it a law. Two tests would move it, and neither has been
  run: readers handed the four seeds but not the frames arriving at a similar seven; and a person
  handed nothing at all arriving near the four seeds. All the convergence so far is transmission:
  three outside model sessions were handed the notation and reconstructed parts of the structure in
  their own words. That is real, bounded, and not the stronger claim.
- **Seven is the count of what has been named, not a derivation.** Other stances are available and
  unnamed (§4). The asymmetry between seeds — three stances on `O`, three on `R`, one on `F`, and on
  `V` only the seat — is in the naming, not the structure.
- **Frames are stances, not types.** Nothing here sorts people. Temperament (§9) is a weighting over
  stances any person can enter; it is not a box.
- **The ring is a working order, not a law of development.** The shadow-to-grain bridges were
  drafted; of the three outside sessions, one returned the frames' oppositions and one a pattern of virtue, shadow, and correction (§7). That is encouraging and it is not a
  test.
- **The operator model is a toy.** It shows that *if* stances read each other's axes, order matters —
  which is a conditional, not a finding about minds.
- **One claim was withdrawn, and one prediction demoted.** Part 1 §6 records that no theorem guarantees a frame its grain, and the withdrawal stands here. The notation's one falsifiable prediction, about change and weight, was demoted in July: it fails if the cycle reading of the grain stands. Part 5 §6 tells it.

## 12 · Open questions

**The lived stretch from Wisdom to Commitment.** Three questions I wrote down in June and have not
answered: *what does it feel like to carry knowledge that was never explained to you? what makes you
keep searching when the inherited answer breaks? how do you know when you've found something real to
commit to, versus just moving out of habit?* An outside model session filed the same gap under
*question versus answer* — some questions are lived through rather than solved. The second-draft
pass on frames four through seven is held until these settle.

**The third graph.** Who curbs whom. Derived from the structure, or intuited and then drawn? Not yet
drawn at all.

**The two sevens.** §5 offers a reading. It is not a ruling. Two working documents disagree on where
Commitment sits — one gives it Decision's question, one files it under `⟨V⟩` — so until the sevens
are reconciled it is the frame most likely to be mis-shelved.

**Stewardship: a frame, or the purpose.** The same files that give Stewardship a seat in the ring
also say it is *not a perspective to invoke* but the reason the others exist. Both are written down;
the ring reading and the purpose reading have not been squared.

**Second frames.** Craft's and Curiosity's are open; Craft is also the only frame the archive's basin notes give no anchor.

**History to weighting.** A way of writing how a perceiver's accumulated history sets which frames
are cheap — the frame-side of Part 1 §9's *history to size*, and the formal piece that would keep
"different perceivers differ" testable rather than circular.

---

## 13 · Closing

The seeds say what a moment is made of. The frames say how a mind stands toward it, and which part
it is holding still so the rest can move. Seven of those stances have names, one seat beneath them
does not get handed off, and the seven answer one another in a ring that the best short description
of them already contains: *distinction becomes relationship, relationship survives change, change
becomes creation, creation becomes care, and care returns to stillness.*

*A lens, not a law.*

## Appendix B · Where the ideas come from

Nothing new is imported as mathematics for the frames beyond what Part 1's Appendix B already lists. The word
*eigenform* and the fixed-point reading are von Foerster's and Kauffman's, used at the strength Part
1 §6 states. The idea that a stance conserves something while everything else moves is a modelling
choice. The shadow pairs and the awareness ladder are the archive's own working vocabulary, and the
word *shadow* is used in its plain sense: the frame's own shape, cast when the light is wrong. §8 leans on one body of standard material. Order effects in judgement — Jennifer Trueblood & Jerome
Busemeyer, "A quantum probability account of order effects in inference," *Cognitive Science* 35
(2011); Busemeyer & Peter Bruza, *Quantum Models of Cognition and Decision* (2012) — show that the
order of evidence changes a judgement. They are about evidence, not about these stances, and support
§8 only at that weaker strength (records and abstracts read, November 2026). Path dependence is named in §8 without a source checked against the page. The return map of a stable cycle belongs to Part 5, with its sources.
