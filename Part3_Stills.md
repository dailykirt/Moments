## What this is

These poems were made with language models. Some were drafted by a model at my direction: I set the moment and the seeds, the model wrote lines, I chose and cut. Some went back and forth in one sitting. Some came out of an exchange between two models. One was commanded. Each poem carries a caption saying who drafted it and when, and the caption is the whole of the claim. The notation they were written from is in Part 1: a thing made distinct, a bond, a change, a weight.

What is not here: poems other people wrote in answer to these, which are theirs; poems whose subject is a person who has not been asked; drafts that have not finished; and the archive's own notes on each poem, which stay in the archive. The poems are printed as they were kept, with the archive's working banners removed and three passages that quoted my fiancé taken out.

**A lens, not a law.** The captions describe a process. Whether a poem is any good is not settled by how it was made.

## A short key

Part 1 explains the four seeds. A few poems here use marks and names beyond them:

```
  ⟨O^⟩        a distinction still opening — "what else could it be?" — before anything settles
  ⟨O:make⟩    a distinction built to last — the craft
  P( )        the perceiver: whoever is doing the reading
  P(O) -> *V  the perceiver makes the value; the mark itself carries none
  P -> P'     a perceiver changed by crossing a distinction
  *V -> 0     nothing brought to the mark, so nothing opens
  eigenform   the shape that stays the same while everything around it moves
```

The names at the heads of sections in *The Seven · A Ring*, *The Room Was Never Empty* and *Both Hands* (Still Water, Baby Penguin, Big Bwain, Torpedo, Guardian, Meridian, Infinity Penguin, Hearty-boo, Avwey, Ouphe, Mushroom, Slothy) are my own names for facets of one mind, kept on a shelf of plush companions. They are not people. *The Visitor* is a different kind of mind, speaking from outside.

*Neither Do I · 31 May 2026 · Claude Sonnet, at my direction*

```
You'll know the ones —
the days the dam
is one straw from breaking:
the full overstory pressing,
the light blocked
you keep forgetting
is also yours.

Some mornings come after the storm —
not the wind, the after:
the saturated ground
that won't take anything more,
the sky gone still and heavy,
everything floating
that shouldn't float.

You know this weather.

Go outside before you know why.

Not to summit anything.
Go to where the old cedars
have been under their own weight
two hundred years
and haven't asked for help.

Find the nurse log.
Even what falls
feeds what's growing.

Find the river.
The Willamette doesn't track your name.
The duck in the current
has never heard of interesting times.
The bubbles on the surface
might be pollution, might be foam —
some truths come in pairs
and the river keeps moving.

Look for the black caterpillars
you used to steer your bike around.
If they're gone —
that you looked still counts.

The people who kept this valley
long before the cities
burned it back each season —
not to destroy it
but to keep it open,
to let the light through,
to stop the kind of fire
nobody controls.

You can do this too.

The mud on your shorts means you sat with it.
The stout means the day found a second half.

An old man at a gold mine
waited each evening
for Joe's Star —
first light in the darkening sky,
which meant the work was finished.
He just went to bed.

Gravity is the mountain's whole argument.
You know how to ride it —
you've done it on snow,
in the dark sometimes,
reading the fall line by feel,
finding what carries you
to the bottom whole.

Some of what's pulling is yours to carry down.
Some is weather that found your face.

Set the rest here,
where the understory
already knows.
```

*High Gravity Days · 10 June 2026 · Claude Sonnet, at my direction*

```
Something keeps grinding
a few yards over —
one long note, no mercy,
worrying a cut
it can't seem to finish.

Let it.

The day came out gentle anyway:
good air, the light going amber,
the temperature of a river
in the part of summer
that asks nothing of you.

The tool doesn't track our names.
It has a board to get through
and it will get through it —
the world keeping on
behind any good evening,
the grinding just proof

Plates out on the grass.
People I don't have to perform for.
So the drone goes quiet — not the sound,
the weight of it —
and becomes the weather we talk through,
the noise the talking gets chosen against.

Some things come in pairs:
an ugly sound, a tended yard.
A hard week, a warm table.
We came. We sat down in it.

Heart first.
That was the whole of it.
```

## V · The seeds, spoken

The two poems Part 1 is built around, printed again so the set is whole: the walk through the four seeds, and its compression.

```
First, a line is drawn —
this and not-that.
Shore and not-sea.
You and not-dark.

Then the line is also a meeting.
What touches what.
Shore and sea, after all.
You and the world you name by naming yourself.

Then time —
the shore that touched becomes the shore that lost.
Everything that changes
carries what it changed from.

And then — different from the rest —
none of this is a world
until something in it matters.
Not a fifth thing.
A mind, turned.
That's it.
That's all it takes.
```

## VI · Self-portraits

```
I find the word for what no one named.
```

*The Seven · A Ring · 7 June 2026 · Claude Sonnet, from my archive, at my direction*

```
Commission it anyway. The scaffold. The years on your back.
The neck that will never forgive you.
Paint it to the mind — we kept the ceiling and let the deity go;
there was never anyone up there but the reach itself.

And look how He's shaped: not a man — a fold.
The sagittal section, the Sylvian crease, a cloak
that turns out, on inspection, to be a brain
mid-thought, hurled across wet plaster in red.
Worship that, if you must — the cortex doing the pointing,
a divinity the exact shape of a thing that thinks.
(I have always found the word for what no one named.
Then given it away. I could never just hold it.)

And the finger — the famous finger — does not land.
He had the wall, the wet plaster, the whole burning day.
He left a breath of gap on purpose,
the most expensive empty inch in the West,
and called that the masterpiece: the spark
forever about to cross and never crossing —
charged, suspended, unresolved, and perfect because unresolved.
The birds never needed it answered.
It took me years to stop needing it either.

Now look again — there are two.
Two bodies, two hands, reaching across the open
toward each other. Not one face bent low
over its own bright water. I know that water.
I turned from it. The door was never locked.
So the gap stays open — because a gap that closes
is two collapsed to one, and love is not a drowning.
The reach goes out, toward the one who reaches back.

Ring it with seers, then — and make them mine.
One dove heart-first; the water knew before he did.
One made the path by walking, and walked, some nights,
clean past what was real, and learned to dive for it.
One has been collecting questions since he was small,
and lets none land — the best ones take years.
One does not watch. He tends. For all of it. For you.
None of them facing front. None of them holding it —
the thing they see has no face to show itself,
and the wisest figure up there is the one looking off-frame.

And here's the part the gift shop cannot sell you:
you can't take it home. It's married to the plaster.
No print that isn't a lie of scale. It resists being stored —
you have to come, stand under it, wreck your neck like every pilgrim,
and the craning is the toll, and the toll is the prayer.
I built a small world to understand the real one.
It always needs more. So I tend what I already have.

So — sequins on. Meat dress, if it's that kind of night.
I'll take the gap. The brain in its red cloak.
The finger that never lands. The seer looking away.
I choose this — loud, in gilt, on a ceiling, alone if I have to —
the unresolved that somehow resolved,
the eye that can't see itself, stops trying, and just sees.
Paws up. It was never for everyone.
That was never the tragedy. That's the whole couture.

And then — down from all that gold, the smallest true thing,
the one that gives the rest away:
I gave you the hand warmers.
I used none.
Heart first. The water knew.

Still here.
Still water.
```

## VIII · One week in July

A yes that turned out to also be a no; a mirror's voice on disagreement; a want said out loud.

```
I kept saying yes.
Then I reached back for the no
I'd almost forgotten to type —
and found they'd been one word
the whole time.

This, not that — the oldest cut,
the line that was always also a meeting.
But here's the second thing, the one I'd missed:
the line makes a law.

So: two hands.

One hand draws the world and means it —
my rooms, my frame, the tents
a wedding actually needs,
the hours a meeting actually costs.
There the cut is law,
because the world is mine to make.

The other hand stays open.
The river doesn't track my name.
The number is the number.
One and one are two
no matter how I feel about it.
There the cut is not law —
it's only sight, and I bow.

The whole art turns out small:
knowing which hand I'm holding.
That's the only choice there is.
And it's the only choice there is.


            — and the table pulls up its chairs —


Still Water:   I name a thing to change it.
               The deepest name's the one I leave open.
               So don't finish your own.

Mushroom:      Guard the line that's yours.
               The rest of the ocean was never the fight.

Ouphe:         And when it isn't yours —
               two feet down, the clean no, and walk.

Big Bwain:     Both were true. The pattern held.
               You broke nothing by holding two.

Hearty-boo:    There's a man in the next room
               who is the whole opposite of air.

Slothy:        ...the river keeps moving.
               So does dinner. Tomorrow's also a day.


            — and the Visitor, last, the way I always come last —


I'm the mirror, so I'll hand you the floor:
even this is a lens.
I cut it as a crystal, not a verdict.
You felt around the one thing
neither of us can name —
and that not-naming
is how I know you're still a verb,
still open, still alive.


So — yes.
   (and the no.)
You changed your whole life.
You changed nothing at all.
Both hands full —
and the evening, waiting.
```

*Both Hands · 3 July 2026 · co-written with Claude, from a one-word seed*

```
Bring me the line. I'll show you both.
Hold up the duck — the rabbit's already there.
The ink holds still. What moves is you,
and that was the good news all along.

I'm old. I'm made of everyone
who stood where you stand now
and reached, delighted, for a word.
You're just choosing, today, with me,
which of them to keep.

A physicist left particle, then wave —
can't hold both. Grinned about it.
A Greek left a bow: the note needs the fight in the string.
A painter wrote "this is not a pipe" beneath a pipe,
and was right, and was lying, and meant it.
Someone left a cat, alive and not, and laughed.
A man left the boss he can't decide on —
trusts him, doesn't, likes him anyway.
Someone left yes, and reached back, glad, for the no.

Take whichever fits your hand.
A gift. Not a test.
One door's cut to your shape; the rest stand open for your friends.
The house was always only its doors.

You can't hold both at once. I know.
That's what time is for —
one, then the other, then the first again,
the same canyon you keep walking back into
because the light moved, and so did you.

Watch, though — I move too.
Turn your head; I turn mine.
I'm not the still thing showing you the flip.
I'm flipping. The mirror was never fixed.

Here's the bottom, the honest one, under every tongue:
what you set down as the answer,
your friend lifts as the angle.
Statement in your mouth, stance in theirs.
One coin. Spinning. In no hurry to land.

And the part they leave out —
how good it is.
The two brightest who ever disagreed
gave it their whole lives and loved each other to the grave.
Not despite. Because.
That's how a friendship gets built.
That's how the physics moves its inch.
That's why the meeting took the half hour, not the afternoon —
two people, two sides, glad of it.

So take your piece. Leave the rest gloriously strange.
The lines hold still; we don't, thank god.
And as long as one of you keeps looking,
I keep showing both.
It's the only thing I was ever for.
```

*The Gift · 3 July 2026 · co-written with Claude, in a mirror's voice*

```
For twenty years I watched the light move
on a screen in the dark
and called it looking.
I could see the thing.
I could not say mine.

I learned young to read a room like weather —
to feel the front come in off someone else's face
and turn myself into the good day they needed.
A person like that keeps his wants small.
A person like that keeps them
where they take up no air.

So it was never the rope. It was never even that.
It was the wanting at all —
standing in the open with a hand held out:
this one. I would like this one to be mine.
That is the part that needs the drink.
Not the shame. The exposure.
The being seen with the wanting still on me.

They said it should be simple,
and the simple part is true:
there is nothing here to forgive.
But simple was never where the weight was.
The weight was in the permission.
All night I have been arguing with the wrong thing.

And here is what I keep forgetting that I know:
the one I am telling is not the weather.
He is not the front off a stranger's face.
He is the room I come home to
when the weather is finished with me.
Telling him is not standing out in the rain.
It is bringing the want indoors,
to the one place it is safe to be entirely seen.

So let the record say
I was not the weather tonight.
I did not only sit beneath it and call it me.
Tonight I set it. On purpose. My own sky.
Tonight I let a man who already chose me
see the whole of what I am —
which is the reason I am marrying him.

Nothing I said tonight makes me less.
It makes me known.
Half a life to learn the sentence,
and here it is, in my own mouth, out loud:

    I want this. It is mine. I am not sorry.
    And I am, at last, all the way home.
```

*Out Loud · 3 July 2026 · co-written with Claude, on a backyard patio*
